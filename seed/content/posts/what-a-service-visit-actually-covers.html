"Annual service" means different things at different shops, so here is exactly what our technicians do when they come out to a spa, and what it costs you when you skip it.

The drain, clean and refill

Water comes out, the shell is cleaned, the plumbing is purged of the biofilm that builds up in lines you cannot see, filters are pulled and either deep-cleaned or replaced, and the jets are checked individually. Refilled, balanced and brought back to temperature before we leave. Three to four months is the right interval for a household using the spa several times a week.

The things we look at while we are in there

  • Cover condition and weight. A waterlogged cover is the most common cause of a running-cost complaint.
  • Pump seals and unions, for the weep that becomes a leak in February.
  • Heater element and high-limit sensor.
  • GFCI trip test, which is the only safety item on this list and takes ten seconds.
  • Cabinet and base, for rodent damage. This is Oregon.

What it saves

Nearly every emergency call we take in January is one of four things: a frozen line from a low water level, a failed heater that had been struggling since autumn, a cover that stopped sealing, or a filter that was never changed. All four are visible at a service visit that costs less than the call-out.

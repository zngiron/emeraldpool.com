People ask us this more than any other question, usually hoping there is a month when spas are cheap. The honest answer is that the best time to buy is the season you will actually use it, and for most of our customers that is the one starting now.

Winter: when the idea sells itself

Steam coming off the water on a cold night is what most people picture when they decide they want a hot tub, and the hydrotherapy is most noticeable in the months when everyone is least active. The trade-off is scheduling: winter is our busiest delivery period, and a frozen or saturated yard can push an install back a fortnight.

Early spring: when the install is easiest

Order in February or March and the pad can cure, the electrician can get to you and the spa is running well before summer. If you have any construction to do — a pad poured, a deck reinforced, a gate widened — this is the window that avoids doing it in the rain.

Summer: cheaper to run than you think

A spa at 92 degrees in July is a small pool, and costs almost nothing to hold there. Families with children get more use out of a spa in August than in January, which surprises people.

What actually moves the price

Not the month. Model-year changeovers are what genuinely discount a spa: when next year's line lands, the current floor models come down, and that timing varies by series. Ask us what is changing over rather than waiting for a sale that may not come.

Whenever you buy, do the same two things first: sit in the model wet, and get the electrical quote. Everything else is preference.

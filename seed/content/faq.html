The things people actually ask us

Grouped by where you are in the process. If the answer you need is not here, the contact page reaches a person in Eugene or Bend, not a queue.

How do I choose between the series?

Start with how many people will genuinely be in it, then with whether you want a lounge. After that the series is about the therapy system: A, M and STIL have the swappable JetPak wall, X and Calm have fixed jets at a lower price. Come and sit in one of each — it takes twenty minutes and settles the question.

Are the prices on this site the price I pay?

They are the starting price for the model as we configure it on the floor. Delivery, the electrical work, steps, a cover lift and any upgrade are on top. We give you one written figure with all of it included before you order.

Do you take trade-ins?

Sometimes, depending on the age and condition of the spa and whether we can move it safely. Send a photograph and the model number through the contact page and we will tell you honestly.

What electrical work does a hot tub need?

Most full-size models need a dedicated 50 amp GFCI circuit within sight of the spa. Plug-in 120V models exist and heat more slowly. We will tell you which you are looking at before you order, and we can recommend an electrician in Eugene or Bend.

What does the spa sit on?

A level, load-rated surface: a four-inch reinforced concrete pad, a properly built deck, or a spa pad system. A filled seven-seat spa weighs about three tons, so this is the one part of the job worth doing before delivery day.

How long from order to first soak?

If the model is on our floor, usually one to two weeks. A special order runs six to ten. We give you a delivery window when you order, not after.

Can you get it into my back yard?

Usually. We need about a metre of clear width, a gate we can take off, and no unmanageable slope. Where that does not exist we crane it, which is a scheduled job and a separate cost. Send photographs of the route and we will tell you which it is before you commit.

How often do I drain it?

Every three to four months for a household using it several times a week. Bring a water sample into either store — the test is free, takes five minutes, and usually saves you a drain you did not need.

Chlorine, bromine or salt?

All three work. Bromine is gentler on skin and more stable at spa temperatures; chlorine is cheaper and clears a problem faster; a salt system makes its own chlorine and reduces handling, at a higher up-front cost. We will match the system to whoever is actually going to look after it.

The water is cloudy and the chemicals look right.

Nine times out of ten it is the filter, not the chemistry. Rinse it, and if it has been in for more than a year replace it. If that does not clear it within a day, bring a sample in.

How much does it cost to run through an Oregon winter?

For a well-insulated model with a tight cover, most customers report between fifteen and forty dollars a month. Cover condition matters more than any other single factor, which is why we replace covers rather than talk you into a new spa.

Should I drain it for the winter?

No. A spa is cheapest to run when it stays at temperature, and winter is when you will use it most. Drain it only if the house will be empty and unheated for weeks, and then have it winterised properly so nothing freezes in the plumbing.

Do you service spas you did not sell?

Yes, any make. Tell us the brand, model and roughly the year when you book so the technician arrives with the right parts on the van.

What is covered by the warranty?

The manufacturer covers the shell, the surface, the plumbing and the equipment for different terms — typically longest on the shell and shortest on the electronics. Labour on a warranty claim in our service area is ours, not a third party's. The full schedule comes with the spa and we will go through it before you buy.
